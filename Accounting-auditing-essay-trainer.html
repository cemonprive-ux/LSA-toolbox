Environmental Accounting Standards

Cette page prépare une réponse ORALE structurée à la question posée sur le texte, pas une dissertation écrite. Vous apprenez une méthode transférable : analyser la question, construire des arguments avec le texte ET vos connaissances, préparer des notes courtes, puis parler à partir de ces notes.

Discussion question
To what extent can new environmental accounting standards help fight climate change?
Source Document

New international sustainability standards board is a 'game-changer'

The International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) Foundation, a not-for-profit international organisation responsible for developing a single set of high-quality global accounting standards, announced the formation of a new International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB) on day three of the COP26. This announcement is a pivotal moment that has the potential to change the way boards and investors make financial investment decisions in the coming years.

Through this new Board, the IFRS Foundation aims to put sustainability reporting on the same footing as financial reporting by establishing a sister body to the International Accounting Standards Board (the IASB Board). It is driven by global investors and is being introduced to give sustainability information the same rigor and comparability that the capital markets expect of financial information.

The intention behind the formation of the ISSB is to develop IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards, a comprehensive baseline that provides a common global reporting framework and transparent information for investors and other stakeholders to determine how sustainability impacts an organisations' financial value. The ISSB framework will enable this by changing how actions around sustainability are reported to the market, how climate and broader sustainability issues impact value in the short, medium, and long term, and the way companies view and manage their strategic risks.

The ISSB will sit alongside and work in close cooperation with the IASB, ensuring connectivity and compatibility between IFRS Accounting Standards and the IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards. The ISSB and the IASB will be independent and overseen by the IFRS Foundation Trustees, who are accountable to a Monitoring Board of capital market authorities.

The standards will be developed to ensure compatibility with requirements that are jurisdiction specific as well as standards which are aimed at a wider group of stakeholders. G20 Leaders and the Financial Stability Board have welcomed the IFRS Foundation's work programme to develop global baseline standards for sustainability disclosures.

The IFRS Foundation will be taking a "climate first" approach leveraging the Taskforce on Climate related Financial Disclosures (TCFD) Guidelines for its first standard. The TCFD, in its role as a strategic risk framework, has acted as a catalyst to challenge Boards on the way they think about risks, stretched their planning horizons, and ultimately driven better strategic decisions. Taking the structure and thinking of the TCFD as a starting point for the first ISSB standard is a very smart move and will allow them to hit the ground running.

The IFRS Foundation Trustees are currently appointing a Chair and Vice-Chair(s) to the ISSB and will commence searching for members to fill the additional board positions.

Given this announcement, you and your organisation should consider whether sustainability is treated as a communications process or as a core strategic risk and opportunity, and whether climate performance is given the attention it needs in this fast-paced and changing environment.

The first IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standard in climate will be a game-changer with the potential to fundamentally change how organisations report their risks and opportunities. Now is the time to prepare.

Source: KPMG Newsroom, "New International Sustainability Standards Board is a 'game-changer'" — Adrian King, 4 November 2021.

04 Connect Document and Knowledge

Un bon argument enrichit le document avec vos connaissances — il ne se contente pas de le paraphraser.

Worked example
From the documentThe ISSB aims to give sustainability information the same rigour and comparability as financial information. texte, §2
From your knowledgeFinancial information is already subject to statutory audit requirements to ensure its reliability. connaissances
Argument you can buildGiving sustainability data the same rigour as financial data is only meaningful if it is also audited with the same rigour — pointing to the need for assurance requirements alongside the ISSB standards.
Your turn.From the document: standards are developed to fit requirements that are "jurisdiction specific", so adoption remains uneven (doc, §5). From your knowledge: think of a binding regional regulation (e.g. the European CSRD) that could complement a voluntary global standard. Build one argument connecting the two.

Exemple possible

ISSB standards remain jurisdiction-specific and largely voluntary, but binding regional rules such as the European CSRD show that mandatory reporting achieves far more consistent adoption — suggesting that global standards work best alongside regional legislation, not instead of it.

05 Build a Strong Argument

Un argument oral efficace suit quatre temps : IDEA → EXPLAIN → EXAMPLE → LINK.

Why this matters
Idea only — too weakNew accounting standards give investors better information.
+ Explain — betterNew accounting standards give investors better information because they make climate information as rigorous and comparable as financial data.
+ Example — stronger…For example, the ISSB's first standard builds on the existing TCFD framework, which has already pushed boards to treat climate as a strategic risk rather than a communications exercise.
Part 1 — Advantages Build one argument

Modèle — Part 1

Idea — New standards push boards to treat climate as a core strategic risk, not just a communications exercise.

Explain — Because the ISSB's "climate first" approach builds on the existing TCFD framework, a proven strategic-risk model that stretches planning horizons.

Example — For example, the initiative is backed by the G20 and the Financial Stability Board, giving it real institutional weight and global scale.

Link — This shows that, by redirecting attention and eventually capital towards climate-responsible companies, the standards can genuinely support decision-making on climate risk.

Part 2 — Limits Build one argument

Modèle — Part 2

Idea — A disclosure standard does not, by itself, cut a single tonne of emissions.

Explain — Because reporting only creates the information that investors could act on — it does not oblige any company to actually reduce its impact.

Example — For example, standards remain "jurisdiction specific", so adoption is uneven across countries, and without strict auditing or penalties, companies may comply on paper only.

Link — This shows that, on its own, disclosure is a necessary but insufficient tool in the fight against climate change.

08 Nuance Your Answer

« To what extent » n'accepte jamais une réponse tout-ou-rien. Utilisez may, can, could, to some extent, depending on, unless…

Too binaryEnvironmental accounting standards will stop climate change.
NuancedEnvironmental accounting standards may support the fight against climate change to some extent, but their real impact will depend on audit quality, regulation and how investors act on the disclosed information.
Rewrite this claim with more nuance:« Sustainability reporting standards will solve the climate crisis. » Utilisez au moins un mot de nuance.

Modèle — nuance

Sustainability reporting standards can help address the climate crisis to some extent, but this largely depends on whether investors act on the disclosed information and whether binding regulation and auditing accompany the standards.

Introduction

New environmental accounting standards are a genuine step forward, but I do not think they can fight climate change on their own. Part 3. Overall assessment

Conclusion

Overall, new environmental accounting standards can support the fight against climate change, but they cannot win it alone. They give investors and boards climate information as rigorous as financial data, and push companies to treat climate as a strategic risk — but a reporting standard cannot force any company to actually cut its emissions. The best outcome combines these standards with binding regulation, rigorous auditing and genuine political will.
