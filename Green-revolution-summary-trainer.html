Green Revolution: 'Nudge Brands & Consumers With Smart Tech To Incite Behavioural Change', Says New Report

A new consumer research report has highlighted "brand peer pressure" and technological innovation as critical to push forward sustainable behaviour among both shoppers and businesses. The new report, conducted by a team of behavioural change and sustainability experts, also puts forward a range of recommendations, among them reminding consumers that "they are not alone when it comes to making sustainable choices".

Released by the British tech-for-good app developer 3 Sided Cube on Monday (March 8), the Igniting A Green Revolution report provides insights into what could help drive and accelerate sustainable brand and consumer behaviour, as well as the role of technology as a tool to promote climate-friendly habits.

One of the key findings from the report highlights the effectiveness of brand peer pressure on businesses to green their operations, suggesting that in addition to financial incentives, companies can be pushed to make more sustainable changes due to moral reasons and pressure from their industry rivals and competitors. According to the analysts, if more waste reduction, reuse and emission reduction initiatives and campaigns are celebrated by governments, other businesses and wider society, it is more likely that companies will "wake up to their responsibilities to become more sustainable".

Another "igniting" factor to push forward sustainable change is technology, which the report describes as a "crucial tool" when it comes to consumers. Based on 3 Sided Cube's previous survey data indicating that the majority of shoppers are motivated to live more sustainable lives and are demanding digital tools to help them make such changes, the research believes that tech innovation will enable "smarter, greener decisions" and "help consumers see how they are part of a growing community focused on green issues".

Commenting on the findings, managing director of 3 Sided Cube, Richard Strachan, said: "Reprimanding people doesn't work if we want to reach a mass audience. We have to 'nudge' consumer and brand behaviour towards greener choices. Technology is one of the best tools we have to make this happen."

Among some of the key issues that consumers are concerned about includes non-recyclable packaging and complex recycling rules. From the businesses' perspective, the report finds that while they recognise their responsibility to take climate action, the "lack of government financial support" and guidance remain key barriers to making sustainable changes.

Recommendations include showing consumers that sustainable lifestyles are "increasing in popularity every day", because shoppers have indicated a greater likelihood to follow through with actions if they believe they are part of a broad trend made up of many individuals.

Source: 3 Sided Cube, "Igniting A Green Revolution" report — cited 8 March.

Step 1 / 7

Understand

Before you can summarise a text, you need to separate two things that students often confuse: what the text is about, and what the author thinks about it.

Topic

The subject the text discusses. Neutral — it doesn't tell you the author's opinion.

Author's position

What the author actually argues or concludes about that topic.

TOPIC ≠ AUTHOR'S POSITION — a good summary always states both, separately.

Step 4 / 7

Reformulate

Four reusable techniques to put ideas into your own words — the skill you'll need for every future summary, not just this one.

4.1 — Synonym

Replace tools with a suitable synonym and rewrite the sentence.
"Technology is one of the best tools we have to make this happen."
Model — Technology is one of the best means we have to make this happen.

4.2 — Change the structure

Rewrite this sentence in the passive voice.
"3 Sided Cube released the Igniting A Green Revolution report."
Model — The Igniting A Green Revolution report was released by 3 Sided Cube.

4.3 — Generalise

These three issues share something in common. Name the category in a few words.
non-recyclable packaging · complex recycling rules · lack of government financial support
Model — practical and regulatory barriers

4.4 — Combine

Merge these three short sentences into one.
"Technology can help consumers make smarter decisions. Technology can help consumers make greener decisions. Technology can help consumers feel part of a community."
Model — Technology can help consumers make smarter, greener decisions and feel part of a wider community.

Model answer

The article examines a report by 3 Sided Cube, a British technology company, on the factors that can accelerate sustainable behaviour among businesses and consumers. The author explains that competitive and moral pressure between rival companies, described as "brand peer pressure", can push businesses to adopt greener practices, alongside ordinary financial incentives. It also points out that digital technology helps consumers make smarter, greener choices and feel part of a wider community working together on environmental issues. Another concern is that businesses face real barriers to change, particularly a lack of government financial support and unclear recycling guidance, while consumers are put off by confusing packaging rules and inconsistent labelling. Finally, the report concludes that nudging people through technology and social proof works better than reprimanding them, and that clearer, more consistent government support remains essential for real, lasting progress.

Summary Method

  1. Understand

    What is the text about? What is the author's position?

  2. Select

    Keep main ideas. Remove unnecessary evidence.

  3. Condense

    Combine several details into one broader idea.

  4. Reformulate

    Use your own words and structures.

  5. Organise

    Build a logical plan.

  6. Write

    Be concise, accurate and neutral.

  7. Check

    Content, selection, reformulation, organisation, language and word count.
